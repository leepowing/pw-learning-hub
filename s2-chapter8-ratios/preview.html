S2 · CHAPTER 8 · SECTION 8.2

Ratios

Compare like quantities, simplify without changing the relationship, and divide a total into equal parts.

MeaningSimplifySharePractise

1 · ORDER MATTERS

Compare quantities of the same kind

A ratio compares quantities by division. For 8 green and 12 purple counters:

Green : purple = 8 : 12 = 2 : 3

For every 2 green counters, there are 3 purple counters. Reversing the order gives purple : green = 3 : 2.

Express quantities in the same unit before writing a unit-free ratio. For example, 2 m : 50 cm = 200 cm : 50 cm = 4 : 1.

Part compared with part

Green : purple = 2 : 3

Green ÷ purple = 2/3.

Part compared with the whole

Green : total = 2 : 5

Green is 2/5 of all the counters.

What fraction of all the counters is purple?

2 · EQUIVALENT RATIOS

Multiply or divide every term by the same factor

For positive quantities, multiplying or dividing all terms by the same positive number preserves the ratio.

2 : 3 = (2 × 1) : (3 × 1) = 2 : 3

Adding the same number does not generally preserve a ratio: 2 : 3 and 3 : 4 are different.

Simplest whole-number form

  1. Convert quantities to the same unit if needed.
  2. Clear fractions or decimals by multiplying every term by the same number.
  3. Divide every term by the greatest common factor.

Whole numbers

24 : 36 = 2 : 3

Divide both terms by 12.

Decimals

0.8 : 1.2 = 8 : 12 = 2 : 3

Multiply by 10, then divide by 4.

Fractions

1/3 : 1/2 = 2 : 3

Multiply both terms by 6.

3 · THREE-TERM RATIOS

Keep every quantity in the correct order

For 10 red, 15 blue and 25 yellow counters:

Red : blue : yellow = 10 : 15 : 25 = 2 : 3 : 5

Divide all three terms by 5. The whole consists of 2 + 3 + 5 = 10 parts.

Combining two ratios

Given a : b = 3 : 4 and b : c = 2 : 5, match the shared quantity b:

a : b = 3 : 4

Keep b as 4 parts.

b : c = 4 : 10

Multiply 2 : 5 by 2.

a : b : c = 3 : 4 : 10

Do not join the original terms as 3 : 4 : 5. The two values representing b must match first.

4 · SHARING A TOTAL

Find one part, then build each share

Total: $100 · Ratio: 2 : 3

  1. Total number of parts = 2 + 3 = 5.
  2. Value of one part = $100 ÷ 5 = $20.
  3. Multiply one part by each ratio term.
For a total T in the ratio a : b:
First share = T × a/(a + b)
Second share = T × b/(a + b)

Check that the shares add back to the original total.

5 · WHEN THE TOTAL IS NOT GIVEN

Decide what the known amount represents

One share is known

A : B = 3 : 4. A has 21 marbles.

3 parts = 21, so 1 part = 7.

B has 4 × 7 = 28 marbles.

The difference is known

A : B = 5 : 2. A has 18 more marbles than B.

3 parts = 18, so 1 part = 6.

A has 30; B has 12.

A total uses the sum of the parts. A difference uses the difference between the parts. A known share uses only that share's number of parts.

7. Share $420 between A and B in the ratio 2 : 5. Enter A's share, then B's share.

Show worked solution

One part = 420 ÷ 7 = 60. A gets $120 and B gets $300.

8. Red : blue counters = 3 : 7. There are 18 red counters. How many blue counters are there?

Show worked solution

One part = 18 ÷ 3 = 6, so blue = 7 × 6 = 42.

9. A : B = 5 : 3 and A has 14 more stickers than B. How many stickers does A have?

Show worked solution

One part = 14 ÷ 2 = 7. A has 5 × 7 = 35 stickers.

10. Green : purple counters = 2 : 3. There are 40 counters altogether. How many are purple?

Show worked solution

40 × 3/5 = 24 purple counters.

SECTION SUMMARY

Same units. Correct order. Equal-sized parts.

  • Apply the same multiplication or division to every ratio term.
  • Use the total number of parts when finding a fraction of the whole.
  • Match the shared term before combining ratios.
  • Identify whether the known amount is a total, a share or a difference.
